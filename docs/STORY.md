# Nightfall District — story and experience record

## Active campaign: Dead Signal

The active campaign now contains exactly three directed chapters: **city streets → abandoned train station → abandoned factory**. Each has a miniboss and a boss. This supersedes the earlier arcade-to-station route while preserving those ideas below. It is an original crossover fan-game mission informed by Jujutsu Kaisen's urban-curtain imagery, not a claim about canon anime events.

An emergency broadcast repeats above Shibuya's deserted crossing. The curtain is drawing power through the abandoned transit line into Kurogane Works, an industrial complex at the route's end. The squad must open the crossing, trace the signal through the empty station and destroy the furnace relay before an evacuation route can be restored. Familiar city signs become transit warnings, then industrial lockout notices: the scenery tells the players they are getting closer to the source.

The emotional through-line remains collective rescue. Naruto's persistence, Luffy's immediate willingness to help and Tanjiro's compassion motivate the crossover team. This mission does not reduce the characters to interchangeable aggressive barks. Their hero identities and playable kits are implementation features; any future character-specific voiced dialogue requires its own actual asset and test record.

## Three chapters, six confrontations

### 01 — City streets / Open the cordon

Commercial signs still glow, but a response van is empty and the traffic signal has failed. **Crosswalk Executioner** guards the crossing, turning the ordinary road-warning silhouette into a threat. **Siren Marshal** holds the evacuation cordon farther down the street. Defeating the Marshal opens the route to Exit 13.

The player learns to recognize attack windups in a familiar, readable space. The crossing stripes make horizontal progress obvious. The first boss should reward avoiding a clear warning and punishing recovery, not memorizing an unseen combo.

### 02 — Abandoned station / Follow the last departure

The train is still here; the passengers are not. A clock has stopped, a kiosk is shuttered and tickets lie beside abandoned luggage. **Platform Widow** patrols beneath the stopped clock. **Last Conductor** stands between the derailed carriage and a signal showing a departure that never happened.

The last-departure board reveals maintenance access to the industrial line. The squad's hope of a quick escape becomes a commitment to reach the signal's source. The station is not the finale, and the mission should not announce total victory here.

### 03 — Abandoned factory / Silence the source

A stopped conveyor feeds the furnace hall. **Furnace Hound** protects the assembly line. **Kiln Sovereign** is the final industrial curse, framed by the barred core of Furnace 03. The fight should feel like the culmination of the route: larger machinery, broader silhouettes and stronger amber illumination, while attack warning colors remain consistent with the earlier stages.

Defeating the Sovereign breaks the relay and makes evacuation possible. A victory screen is valid for the current slice. A working train escape, moving crane set piece or rescued-civilian procession must not be described as implemented until those interactions exist.

## Experience constraints

Keep the route playable during story delivery. Use short barks, readable changing objectives and environmental clues instead of long exposition cutscenes. Every miniboss/boss is a distinct encounter milestone, and the player should understand what opened after defeating it. The team must not be split by an unexplained gate, teleported into a hostile arena without feedback or held in an idle dialogue scene.

## Deferred mechanics retained

Civilian rescue, a paired relay activation, a train departure after the final victory and named-hero banter remain preserved ideas. Their implementation status is separate from this narrative intent. The industrial curse can point to a later district without expanding this three-stage mission into a sandbox.

# Nightfall District — story and experience record

## Canon reference versus authored fiction

The setting draws from Jujutsu Kaisen's Shibuya Incident imagery: a city trapped by a curtain, multiple entry routes, commercial streets, underground transit and abrupt transitions from everyday signage to supernatural danger. The actual mission below is original crossover fan-game fiction, not a retelling or a claim about canon chronology. The in-scene time of 21:31 is atmospheric and does not claim that these gameplay events occurred at that time in the anime.

## Mission: Last Train Out

An emergency message repeats above a silent crossing. A curtain has trapped the district and cut its routes apart. Three relay seals keep the station barrier active. The players form a temporary rescue team, fighting across the crossing and shopping arcade to reopen a station evacuation route before the last train is lost.

The dramatic question is cooperative: can everyone make it through together? The crossover cast can disagree in voice and style while aligning on that immediate purpose. Naruto's persistence, Luffy's direct willingness to help, and Tanjiro's compassion are requested character directions to preserve when actual licensed or appropriately authorized character assets and dialogue are developed. Avoid giving all three interchangeable gruff combat barks.

## Beat sheet

1. **Crossing / orientation.** Neon commercial signs flicker through the emergency broadcast. A visible seal ahead explains why the team cannot simply run to the finish. The first group teaches spacing and launching through play. On victory, the route opens and the presentation should clearly reward forward movement.
2. **Arcade / people worth saving.** Warm lanterns and closed shops imply interrupted lives. Proposed future interaction: free a trapped shopkeeper or restore an emergency relay while another player holds off attackers. The design should never require an idle player to watch a lengthy dialogue scene.
3. **Station / commitment.** Suspended-service boards and the stopped train reveal the mission's destination. The final enemy defends the curtain. Its major attack telegraphs must remain visible when several players use their abilities simultaneously.
4. **Escape / payoff.** Planned narrative payoff: the seal breaks, white station lights return and the train becomes the route out. The shipped slice may currently use a victory state; do not represent a working train sequence or civilian rescue as implemented until it exists and has been tested.

## Narrative constraints

Player agency should remain movement and combat throughout the stage. Story arrives through short barks, changing signs, scenery and immediate objectives. Avoid an opening exposition cutscene. The player should see the next landmark after each gate opens. Defeat should restart a readable checkpoint with a fast return to cooperative play. Never require teammates to perform an uncommunicated sequence to advance.

## Preserved future threads

The origin of the crossover disturbance can become a later chapter. Rival factions should arrive after the rescue loop works. Each new anime roster entry needs a role within the team, a clear combat identity and a reason to help; adding names alone does not deliver the crossover fantasy. A boss rematch can reveal that the three relays were one node in a larger transit-network curse, supporting another directed mission without turning this project into a sandbox.
