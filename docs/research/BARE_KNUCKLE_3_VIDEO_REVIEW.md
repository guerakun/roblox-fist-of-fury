## Exact reference and access status

Requested video: [Bare Knuckle III (Genesis) Playthrough [English] — NintendoComplete](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZD3M7MmDdrA).

The exact title and author were verified using YouTube's public [oEmbed metadata](https://www.youtube.com/oembed?url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZD3M7MmDdrA&format=json). Metadata confirms the source identity; it does not establish its gameplay, duration, difficulty, selected route or controller inputs.

Initial browser/web access failed, but the integration reviewer subsequently recovered and decoded the exact video locally (reported duration 74:30, decoded resolution 480×360). This reviewer then **directly inspected 50 frames at 30-second intervals covering approximately 00:15–24:45**, plus five dense 12-second sequences at two frames per second. This is sampled visual review, not continuous viewing of all 74 minutes and not an audio review.

Temporary contact sheets and the source video remain under the ignored `build/` directory. No video file or copied gameplay image is included in this research document or the production project. Contact-sheet timestamps are approximate, with dense-sequence uncertainty of roughly one sample (0.5 seconds). A sampled sequence supports the visible action; it does not reveal the controller input or exact frame data.

## Official game mechanics, separate from video observations

SEGA's [official Bare Knuckle III page](https://vc.sega.jp/vc_bknuckle3/) documents the following. These are corroborated game mechanics, **not timestamped findings from the requested upload**:

| Officially documented feature | Curtain Break source comparison |
| --- | --- |
| Two-player simultaneous play | **Implemented, with a different scope:** the current project supports up to four co-op players (`Config.MaxPlayers`). This does not prove final four-player runtime performance. |
| A charged special can avoid a health cost | **Different:** hero specials currently use fixed cooldowns. There is no equivalent health-spend/recharged-safe-special economy. |
| Score upgrades dash attacks through three levels; a lost life reduces the level | **Missing equivalent:** Dash is a movement/escape action. Earned chapter XP and a single boon are separate progression systems, not score-evolved dash attacks. |
| Weapon-specific specials consume weapon durability | **Missing equivalent:** there is no gameplay weapon-pickup, carried-weapon inventory or durability system. The baseline blade-kit prop and enemy weapons are character presentation, not collectible equipment. |
| Scenario branching | **Missing equivalent:** the campaign follows the fixed city → abandoned station → abandoned factory sequence. |
| Encounter-dependent secret character recruitment | **Missing equivalent:** SEGA describes freeing the kangaroo by defeating its handler first. Curtain Break offers its three heroes directly; it has no comparable conditional recruitment encounter. |

The official [Japanese instruction manual](https://www.sega.jp/genesismini2/jp/assets/manual/pdf/JP_Streets-of-Rage-3.pdf) is retained as a primary reference for a later move-by-move check. Its existence is verified; this revision does not attribute unseen manual pages or translated control details to it.

## Current Curtain Break gameplay inventory for the video comparison

The following is a source audit alongside the sampled video, not a claim that every feature has completed normal-input multiplayer acceptance. “Implemented” means the behavior exists in production source. “Partial” means a related behavior exists but the specified depth is not present. “Missing” means no corresponding production action/system was found in this audit. Items confirmed above use the timestamped visual evidence. Remaining questions are not assertions about unobserved reference behavior.

| Comparison point to inspect in the video | Current Curtain Break state | Concrete source evidence / limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Left-to-right lane movement with depth alignment | **Implemented.** Horizontal travel plus bounded Z movement, with jumping on Y. | `Config.LaneMin/LaneMax`, `WorldBuilder` lateral boundaries and `Main.client` movement. It is a controlled corridor, not a sandbox. |
| Clear a fight, then advance into the next fight | **Implemented.** Three stages, four encounters each; all surviving players rally forward. | `EncounterService.waitForTraverse`, `waitForRally`, and the twelve configured encounter entries. Four/five-second intermissions produce deliberate pauses; the 10:09.5–10:11 forward-arrow sequence is a concrete pacing reference, without establishing an entire-stage average. |
| Camera framing and screen-lock rhythm | **Partial comparison.** Fixed side/elevated angle, smoothed party framing and distance changes; server progression uses world-space limits. | `Main.client` `NightfallPresentation`, `Combat.SetWalkingLimit`. This is not proof of matching the reference's camera locks or composition. |
| Enemy entry and enclosure | **Implemented basic groups; limited staging variety.** Each encounter spawns its configured group around a fixed landmark. | `EncounterService.run` creates a complete group before Combat begins. The waterfront arrivals at 10:15.5–10:18.5 and motorcycles at 13:30–13:41.5 show staging absent from these fixed group spawns. |
| Repeated strikes and a stronger sequence finish | **Implemented.** A three-step Light chain gets a stronger third strike. | `CombatService.performAttack`: continuation within 0.9 seconds; third hit increases damage, knockback and lift. The current animation route alternates Light/Light2; it is not an expansive authored per-character combo tree. |
| Jump attacks and air control | **Partial.** Jump and one airborne Recovery exist, and attacks can be requested while airborne. | `actionReceived` Jump/Recovery plus common attack handling. The reference shows an authored airborne kick at 05:41.5–05:42.5; Curtain Break has no separate directional jump-kick, aerial combo or landing-tech action. |
| Defensive escape versus facing | **Implemented, with its own rules.** Directional guard, guard break and cooldown dash with a short invulnerability window. | `ApplyHit` checks guard facing; `actionReceived` implements Dash/Block. Do not label this identical to any reference roll or special escape before observing it. |
| Grabbing, holding, throwing, vaulting or using one enemy against another | **Missing.** No Grab/Throw/Vault action or held-enemy state was found. | `CombatService.allowedActions` and hit processing. Launch knockback alone is not the held-enemy throw visible at 05:48–05:49.5. |
| Breakable scenery as a reward source | **Partial.** Heavy/special attacks break nearby complete prop assemblies, which restore after 20 seconds. | `performAttack`, `DestructionService.BreakNearby`. Props currently provide spectacle; they do not reveal food as the tire stacks do at 10:11.5–10:12, or drop other gameplay pickups. |
| Sustained health, lives and recovery pickups | **Different survival model.** Percent accumulates toward stronger launches; stocks and ring-out/percent thresholds determine defeat. | `CombatMath.Knockback`, `InBlastZone`, `Config.PlayerPercentLimit`, `knockOut`. No health-food pickup economy was found. This follows the user's Smash-inspired requirement. |
| Co-op collision, assistance and friendly damage | **Implemented with explicit choices.** Same-team damage is rejected; ShareStock can revive an eligible teammate by transferring a stock. | `ApplyHit` team check and `Combat.ShareStock`. The reference video's co-op/friendly-fire behavior is not yet observed here. |
| Boss pressure, openings and phase escalation | **Implemented.** Six original elites have move patterns, floor warnings, armor/poise and stagger recovery. | `Config.Enemies`, `EnemyMoves`, `beginEnemyAttack`, `ApplyHit`. Source presence does not establish readable telegraphs or enjoyable encounter balance on a real device. |
| Environmental hazards and stage-specific traversal | **Partial.** Enemy-created attack footprints provide hazards; scenery changes substantially between chapters. | Boss move definitions and three `WorldBuilder` districts. The playable floor remains continuous and flat; no gameplay elevators, conveyors, collapsing floors or platforming routes were found. |
| Story beats, route choices and ending outcomes | **Partial presentation, linear structure.** Stage intros, checkpoints and final victory exist; authored story is retained in documentation. | `EncounterService` intro/checkpoint/victory events and `docs/STORY.md`. A story document is not an in-game cinematic or a branching objective. |
| Score pressure and replay mastery | **Partial.** Run results track kills/damage/coins/time; persistent chapter rewards support repeat play. | `freshStats`, progression modules. No arcade high-score table, stage timer failure or score-based move evolution was found. |

## Remaining questions requiring closer viewing or play

1. How long does an ordinary skirmish last between first contact and the forward prompt, and how much of that time is movement versus attacking?
2. How often do enemies approach from behind or through authored entrances, and what visual signal precedes each entry?
3. Which inputs and constraints distinguish grabs, throws, air attacks and weapon moves? Their visible use is now confirmed; exact controls, costs and invulnerability are not established by these sampled sequences.
4. What happens when the player consumes the food revealed by a broken stack, and how much does it restore? The break-to-food sequence is confirmed; the recovery amount is not.
5. When does the camera stop, and how far can fighters separate while all relevant threats stay visible?
6. What changes between a normal enemy group, a set piece and a boss beyond enemy health?
7. Which stage geometry changes actual movement decisions, rather than only changing background art?

These questions preserve the user's reference and potential future discussion. They are not themselves an implementation list. The subsequent launch plan selects the approved v1 subset; unanswered research details and other ideas remain preserved without expanding overnight scope.
